# Executive Summary — Sales & Profitability Review
*US business, 2023-2026 dataset years (synthetic). Revenue 2,296.9k, profit 286.4k, margin 12.5%.*

## What happened?
- **Revenue grew strongly** (29% in 2025, 20% in 2026), with the increase coming from more orders rather than larger orders.
- **Margin improved to 13.4% in 2025, then slipped to 12.7% in 2026**, while the effective discount rose from 18.4% to 19.9%.
- **Profit is concentrated:** a quarter of products earn 92% of profit; the top 10% of customers earn 58%.
- **Losses are concentrated:** 79 state x sub-category discount rules lose 145.7k (93% of all line-level losses). Every rule above 40% discount loses money. No product loses money at full price.
- **Furniture** generates 32% of revenue but 6% of profit.

## Why might it matter?
- The business gives away 566.6k in discounts, 2.0x its profit; 20% of revenue is sold below the product's break-even price.
- The data shows **no sign that deep discounts bring extra volume or growth**, but it also cannot prove they do not: discount rules never vary or change, so there is no comparison group. This is association, not cause.
- Discount appears to be set centrally by state and sub-category, so the losses sit in **rules management can change**.
- Regional and customer rankings mostly mirror these rules; judging managers or customers on raw margin would be misleading.

## What should management investigate?
1. **The discount rule table:** who sets each state x sub-category rule, and why 11 states never sell at full price.
2. **The 32 rules above 40%** (99.6k lost), then loss-making 21-40% rules.
3. **Furniture economics:** list price, purchase cost and freight for Tables and Bookcases.
4. **A controlled pilot:** lower the deepest rules in a few states, keep comparable states unchanged, and measure orders, units and profit for two quarters.
5. **Scorecards:** use mix-adjusted margin for regions; protect coverage of top accounts.

## What additional data would be useful?
- Product cost, purchase price and freight (to explain thin Furniture margins).
- Discount rule documentation and approval records.
- Results of a controlled discount pilot (the only way to measure volume effects).
- Competitor prices and promotion calendar; line-level returns; customer history, acquisition and cost to serve; budget or targets.

*Caveat: the dataset is synthetic and its profit appears mechanically generated; findings identify where to investigate, not guaranteed outcomes.*
